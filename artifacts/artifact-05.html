Artifact 5 — AI Leadership, Ethics & Responsible Integration Framework

This final artifact brings together three major pieces of work from Workshop 6: an ethical AI incident analysis, a commercial AI newsletter, and my leadership framework. Together, they represent how I think about AI as both a technical and leadership responsibility.

Section 1 — Ethical Evaluation: Amazon Hiring AI Incident

I evaluated Incident 37 from the AI Incident Database, where Amazon’s experimental hiring model showed gender bias by ranking male candidates higher than female candidates. The issue emerged because the AI learned patterns from historical resumes that reflected previous male-dominant hiring trends.

  • Biased training data: Historical patterns were encoded into the model.
  • Lack of transparency: Applicants did not know an AI system was influencing decisions.
  • Real-world harm: Biased rankings can impact careers and long-term opportunities.

My recommendations included dataset audits, bias testing, transparent disclosures, and strict human oversight.

Section 2 — Commercial AI Across Productivity, Healthcare, and Retail

This newsletter examined how AI is being used commercially right now and how it is reshaping business operations across three industries.

1. Productivity — Microsoft Copilot

Copilot is now widely used for summarization, document drafting, spreadsheet analysis, and workflow automation. It is becoming a digital coworker that boosts productivity and reduces repetitive tasks.

2. Healthcare — DeepMind MedLM

MedLM supports clinicians by generating summaries of medical histories, explaining imaging data, and helping with diagnostic reasoning. It enhances clinical decision-making rather than replacing physicians.

3. Retail — Amazon Forecasting Models

Updated demand forecasting and personalization engines allow retailers to reduce waste, optimize inventory, and predict customer needs more accurately.

Across all three domains, AI is shifting from a tool to a fully integrated decision partner.
